# Buyer review of the v0 home page copy

Date: 8 November 2026. By `customer-persona-reviewer`, who read `docs/copy/v0-home-copy.md`. This is simulated feedback, not market evidence. Confirm it with 5 to 10 real conversations. P1 is a founder or CTO of a 5 to 20 person electronics team, P2 a head of engineering at a 50 to 300 person company, P3 an IP-conscious procurement or legal reader.

## 1. Five second test

- **Headline A.** A solo enclosure designer for teams without a mechanical engineer. P1 sees themselves. P2 reads "no mechanical engineer" as "not me". P3 is glad there is no speed claim.
- **Headline B.** Production hand-off for teams that have engineers. P1 is not the audience ("when your own mechanical engineers are busy"). P2 likes that it admits they have engineers but doubts one person can carry a hand-off. P3 notes that drawings go to third parties.
- **Headline C.** Honest about catalogue boxes. P1 finds the opener blog-like, yet "Sealing, heat, an odd board outline" describes their week. P2 and P3 find it fine but ask for whom.
- **Who replies.** P1 on A. P2 maybe on B, after a referral. P3 prefers A. Suggestion: move C's "When a standard box fits, I say so" next to A's button.

## 2. Earlier objections

- **Fixed:** "we", "build", "senior", the speed promise, the AI line.
- **Partly fixed:** solo-supplier risk (native CAD arrives at the sprint, but there are no milestone payments and no backup person, and capacity is bracketed). Prices and prototype part costs ("you pay manufacturers directly" is bracketed and no price exists). Footer paperwork (the slots exist and are all empty).
- **Not fixed:** proof under the headline (the only image says "Not a customer project"). A findable brand (searching "Normek" shows other firms and no CVR number separates this one).

## 3. What would stop a reply

Would they send the form today? P1 maybe, P2 maybe, P3 no, email first.

1. **P1: "a written scope fixes duration and price before work starts."** They have a ship date and thin cash, and "Request a feasibility review" reads like an order. Fix: a fixed price and duration for the review alone.
2. **P1 and P2: "I show it only with their written permission."** Respectful, but it is no proof. SkyGrids does not show that the author can seal a box. Fix: one real, labelled prototype photo.
3. **P3 and P2: "wording needs a lawyer", plus no legal name, CVR number or insurance.** They cannot verify the firm. Fix: publish the NDA and an ownership sentence and fill the footer, or cut the lines.
4. **P3 and P1: "RFQ pack for [2 to 3, TO CONFIRM] manufacturers."** Whose manufacturers, under whose NDA, and do I approve them? The brief says none are agreed. Fix: say who chooses, and that drawings go only to manufacturers the customer approves, if true.
5. **P2: "university study, not production work."** Honest, yet headline B sells production hand-off, and a manufacturer's review comes from the party selling the tooling. Fix: name an independent check, or say there is none.

## 4. Boilerplate or machine-written

- "A standard box fits until it does not." Slogan cadence.
- "from prototype to production hand-off". Every consultancy says it.
- "Led by Daniel Hessellund Johansen" beside an "I" voice hints at a team. Say plainly that it is one person.
- "Prototype sprint". It promises speed that the copy refuses to promise.

## 5. Which [TO CONFIRM] gaps hurt most

Worst first: (1) legal name, CVR number and address. (2) "wording needs a lawyer", an internal note that says no NDA exists. (3) Insurance. (4) Who pays for prototype parts, shipping and tooling. (5) The LinkedIn link and the photo (the brief says photos exist). (6) Reply time, project cap and manufacturer count. Rule: cut the sentence, never show the bracket.

## 6. Expected but not found

- A plain "I work alone" and who covers if the engineer is ill.
- Who prints the prototypes and who finds the manufacturers, or whether the customer can use their own.
- Who owns the CAD files and drawings after payment (only a footer placeholder today).
- Which CAD system "native CAD" means.
- Where Normek is based (only Aalborg University appears).

## 7. "Not for now" and Data

- **Not for now:** reassuring. "Making parts. Manufacturers make them" and "I build prototypes and measure them" show the author knows their limits, and P2 gets a clean filter. But "dual-use" is undefined, and "Mains-powered or high-voltage products [TO CONFIRM]" may exclude many of the rail-mounted units the page says it takes on.
- **Data:** reassuring about the form. Named processors and "No file upload" are right. Alarming by omission: it covers a message, not STEP files. Where are files kept after the NDA, who prints, who gets the RFQ? Suggested line: "Ask me which services touch your files. I answer in writing before you send anything." It claims no tools and fits the rule to answer honestly.

## 8. Better than the competitors

Each stage lists "You get" and "I need", and stage 3 adds "Not included". Freelancer and print-service pages the reviewer knows only list what they do. "A frozen board revision" shows the author has run this before, and the list filters bad leads.

## Scores

Clarity, credibility, relevance, risk reduction, urgency. P1 5, 3, 4, 3, 3. P2 4, 2, 2, 2, 1. P3 4, 2, 3, 3, 2. Real prospects may skip the footer, weigh price and timing more, and buy on a referral. P2 is not the v0 audience.

## What the manager does with it

**Adopt in one copy pass** (after the fact-check by `mechanical-domain-expert` lands, so the copywriter edits once):

- Plain one-person line in the hero in place of "Led by": one engineer, named.
- Move "If a standard box fits, I recommend it" next to the hero button.
- Add the Data line "Ask me which services touch your files. I answer in writing before you send anything."
- Replace "Prototype sprint" with a name that promises no speed (the copywriter proposes, the manager picks).
- In stage 3, say that manufacturers are chosen with the customer and that drawings go only to the ones the customer approves `[TO CONFIRM]`.
- Add "I work alone" and a `[TO CONFIRM]` line for cover when ill, the CAD system, where Normek is based, and who prints the prototypes.
- Define "dual-use" in a few words, and reword the mains exclusion so DIN rail products on low voltage are not shut out `[TO CONFIRM]`.

**Needs Daniel** (added to `docs/open-questions.md` section 8):

- A fixed price and duration for the feasibility review alone. The simulated P1 would reply more readily.
- One real, labelled prototype photo of his own work.
- Whether an independent molding or tooling engineer can give a second opinion.
- The CAD system he delivers in, and the city he works from.

**Not adopting:** hero B and C stay as test variants. The five person phone test with real people decides, not this review.
