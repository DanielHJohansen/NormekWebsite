# Simulated buyer review of the message (2026-11-08)

**Simulated feedback from `customer-persona-reviewer`. This is not market evidence.** Confirm anything important with 5 to 10 real conversations: mostly technical founders, two engineering heads and one procurement or counsel reviewer.

Inputs were the strategy consult and the open questions. The three personas are a technical founder or CTO of a small electronics startup, a head of engineering at an established industrial electronics firm, and an IP-conscious reviewer.

## 1. Headline picks

- **Founder or CTO: candidate 1.** "Your circuit board works. We build the box it ships in." Snags: "We" sounds like a team, and "build" suggests production boxes. Candidate 2 appeals most, but "[X] weeks" from an unknown solo firm sounds like the promise that burned this persona before. Candidate 3 sounds like the freelancer already tried.
- **Head of engineering: candidate 3, reluctantly.** The subhead "production hand-off for sensors, gateways and controllers" shows the firm knows what follows the prototype. This persona has a mechanical engineer who is overloaded, so "missing" is wrong. Candidate 1's "without in-house mechanical engineers" signals that the offer is not for them.
- **IP-conscious reviewer: candidate 1.** It has no speed claim. "[X] weeks" prompts the question of what is fed into what to get that speed, and "manufacturing partners" prompts the question of who else sees the files. Borrow "led by a named engineer" from candidate 2, because an accountable person is needed.

## 2. The AI disclosure plan

The structure reassures: outcome first, then a page, then a contract clause. Discovering AI use later would be worse. The weak piece is the homepage line "A senior engineer leads every project. AI tools speed up the routine work under written data rules."

- **Founder:** fine. Cares that files are safe and the date holds.
- **Head of engineering:** "senior" next to a founder whose degree ended in 2021 looks off, and if the LinkedIn gap shows, the rest is discounted. "I sign off every drawing myself" means the author checks the author.
- **IP-conscious reviewer:** "routine work" and "written data rules" are vague enough to alarm. Still to be asked: which tools, what retention, where data sits, who at the vendor can read it, the liability cap, and whether a no-AI mode covers built-in CAD features, transcription and email. "Not used for training" is not "not retained". A no-AI surcharge reads as a fine for being careful.

## 3. The offer

Would they contact a one-person firm?

- **Founder:** yes, for the Review. A one-week fixed price credited against the Sprint is easy to approve, and "we recommend a standard box when it fits" is the best trust line in the plan.
- **Head of engineering:** maybe, for a side project, not for a certified product.
- **IP-conscious reviewer:** only after a signed mutual NDA.

Price signals that make them leave:

- "2 to 4 weeks" and DKK 30,000 to 60,000 are both 2x ranges. A "from" price that doubles after scoping reads as bait.
- Silence on who pays for prototype parts, shipping the board, or a third round if "one or two prototype rounds" fails.
- A Production Package "quoted" with no anchor hides the big number. An unstated partner margin reads as hidden markup.
- A price so low that it implies no insurance. The plan lists insurance as still pending.

Proof needed before sending files or money:

- **Founder:** a redacted sample Review, one reference design with photos of a real prototype on a real board plus the build log, one reference call, milestone payments, and native CAD at each stage so a vanished supplier still leaves a usable box.
- **Head of engineering:** a company number, an insurance certificate, a sample drawing package and a named backup.
- **IP-conscious reviewer:** an NDA before any file, IP assignment wording and a tool list that matches the contract. Discounted pilots traded for case-study permission are alarming, so make publication optional and anonymous.

## 4. A cold LinkedIn message

Gets a reply: Daniel under his own name, naming the product and what he saw (a catalogue box, a sealing or connector problem), one linked reference design near the device with its build log, and one easy question such as "Is the enclosure still open?". "If a standard box fits, I'll say so" helps. One line on SkyGrids is fine, but aerospace does not prove the ability to seal a box. The head of engineering answers referrals, not strangers.

Gets ignored: "AI-powered" anything, a calendar link or prices in message one, "10x faster", a template, and a brand that cannot be found (searching "Normek" shows other companies).

## Five highest-impact fixes

1. **Proof before copy.** Put one reference design with its build log and a redacted sample Review directly under the headline. Until then the visitor is asked to trust a stranger.
2. **Remove solo-supplier risk in writing.** Milestone payments, native CAD at each stage, a start date, the projects-per-quarter line, and who steps in if Daniel is unavailable [TO CONFIRM].
3. **Rewrite the AI line to state the rule, not the benefit.** No free or personal accounts, tools linked, a no-AI mode with its price decided now. Drop "senior" unless his record supports it.
4. **Show prices with scope.** What is included, what moves the price, the ceiling, the exclusions, the partner margin and a worked Production Package example.
5. **Put the paperwork in every footer.** Registration number, address, insurance status ("in progress" if true), a downloadable mutual NDA and an IP assignment statement.

**Test first:** candidate 1, with "led by a named engineer" added to the subhead and the verb "build" checked against what is true (design, prototype, partner hand-off). Candidate 2 cannot be tested until a measured number exists. Keep candidate 3 as the challenger.

**Where real prospects may differ:** many will not care about AI at all (the consult itself calls the IP fear a hypothesis), may ignore prices until they see a deliverable, and may buy on a referral, not a headline.

## What the manager does with it

- **Headline order:** candidate 1 amended first, candidate 3 as the challenger, candidate 2 parked until a measured number exists.
- **Voice:** no implied team and no "senior". Say what is true: a named engineer designs and prototypes, and manages manufacturing partners.
- **v0 content:** a plain "what I take on and what I do not" section, the direct email, and footer placeholders for registration, insurance, NDA and IP assignment. It carries no AI line, no prices and no proof section until each is real.
- **The bottleneck is proof.** After the name is cleared, reference design 1 with its build log and a redacted sample Review are the two most valuable assets.
