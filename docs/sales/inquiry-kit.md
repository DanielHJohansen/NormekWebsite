# Inquiry handling kit

8 November 2026, for Daniel. `[TO CONFIRM]` marks a missing fact, `[YOUR PRICE]` and `[YOUR HOURS]` numbers only you can set, `[LAWYER]` a point that needs a lawyer. Every template starts "Hi [first name]," and ends "Daniel".

**For every inquiry**, from the form or your network: check fit (section 2), reply inside the reply time with A, B or a decline, and log it (section 7). Give no firm price or speed before a written scope exists, and claim no client or result you cannot show. **Settle first:** reply time, project cap (defaults in open question 22) and two or three call slots.

## 1. The first reply

Send within [1 working day, TO CONFIRM]. Never ask for files. Keep three or four questions, dropping any already answered.

**A. The sender described a good fit**

> Thank you for writing. What you describe is work I do. Next is a 30 minute call to check fit, then a written scope with deliverables, duration and price. Nothing starts until you accept it.
>
> 1. Is the layout frozen, or can the outline and connectors still move?
> 2. Have you tried a standard box, and what stopped it?
> 3. When do you need the first enclosures in hand?
> 4. Who decides on this, and is a budget set?
>
> No files yet, please, and keep confidential details out of email. [Once the NDA exists: A mutual NDA comes first for files.]
>
> Would [slot 1] or [slot 2] suit you for the call?

**B. The sender gave too little to judge**

> Thank you for writing. I cannot judge fit yet, and four answers will settle it. If it looks right, a 30 minute call comes next, then a written scope.
>
> 1. What does the product do, where is it used (water, dust, temperature) and how is it powered?
> 2. What stage is the board in: layout in progress, frozen with assembled boards, or built?
> 3. Roughly how many units a year, at launch and in year three?
> 4. When does it need to ship?
>
> No files yet, please, and keep confidential details out of email. [NDA sentence as in A.]
>
> Reply by email, or pick a call: [slot 1] or [slot 2].

## 2. Qualification

- **Board stage.** Take on: layout in progress, or frozen with assembled boards. Scope down: layout still moving, so Stage 1 only. Decline: no layout yet.
- **Environment and ingress.** Take on: sealed, outdoor or dusty, with an IP rating to design for. Decline: hot high-pressure washdown (IPX9, IP69K).
- **Volume.** Take on: low-volume devices. Scope down: very few units, so no Stage 3. Decline: above your top limit [TO CONFIRM].
- **Ship date.** Take on: the work ([YOUR HOURS]) fits, with print and manufacturer lead times. Scope down: too tight, so Stage 1 first. Decline: it cannot be met.
- **Who signs.** Take on: a named person who decides and can sign for tooling and orders. Scope down: none yet, so no Stage 3. Decline: nobody can decide.
- **Mains or high voltage.** Take on: low voltage inside, mains only on an external adapter [TO CONFIRM]. Decline: 50 V AC or 75 V DC or more inside [TO CONFIRM, open question 24].
- **Medical, automotive, defense or military use, explosive atmospheres.** Decline for now.
- **Other "not for now" asks.** Scope down: they or a lab handle certification and tests, a prototype replaces simulation, manufacturers make production parts, they buy glands and vents [TO CONFIRM]. Decline: simulation or certification is the whole job.
- **Red flags.** Scope down: requirements moving against a fixed date, so Stage 1 only. Decline: free design first, a big scope with no budget, unclear ownership of the board design.

**Decline, naming the reason**

> Thank you for the detail. Work on [reason, such as medical devices or explosive atmospheres] is on my not-for-now list, so I am not the right engineer for this one. I would rather say so now than start work I cannot do properly. If the scope changes, write again.

**Decline with a referral,** only for someone you would hire

> Thank you for the detail. Work on [reason] is on my not-for-now list, so I am not the right engineer for this one. [NAME AND COMPANY, TO CONFIRM] works on [AREA] and may fit better. [I get no fee for the introduction, TO CONFIRM.]

## 3. The 30 minute call

**Agenda.** Open, 3 minutes: you work alone and take no files today. Product, 12: the questions below. Fit, 7: which stage first, and whether a standard box fits. Next steps, 5: the NDA, when the scope arrives. Close, 3: repeat back, agree one dated next step.

**Questions, in words, per stage "I need" list**
- **Stage 1, feasibility review.** Connector and antenna positions, fixed or movable? Watts, typical and peak? Environment: temperature, water, dust, sun, chemicals, shock? Volume at launch and in year three? If a box failed, what happened? The board model waits for the NDA.
- **Stage 2, design and prototype.** Board revision frozen, and when? One or two assembled boards for me? Parts lists? Who reviews my designs, and can they answer in [2 working days, TO CONFIRM]? Who pays for prototype parts and shipping (default: them, at cost, [LAWYER])?
- **Stage 3, production package.** Prototype accepted? First order and annual volume? Target unit price and tooling budget? Who signs for tooling and orders? Manufacturers in mind? They approve the list before files go out [TO CONFIRM: may they name their own?].
- **Always.** References: none yet, so say so and show only cleared work.

**Price and duration before a scope exists.** Say: "I give no number before I have a written scope, because a guess helps neither of us. The scope fixes deliverables, duration and price before work starts." If they need a range, say: "My range is [YOUR PRICE], an estimate and not an offer." Never quote the strategy documents' ranges, which are untested. Do not say "one week" (C-009, C-010): the fact check puts Stage 1 at 15 to 30 hours, so 1 to 3 weeks at the assumed 10 to 15 hours a week.

## 4. NDA flow

**Now, with no lawyer-reviewed mutual NDA.** Ask for the product in words only. Say it plainly: "I do not have a lawyer-reviewed mutual NDA yet, so I cannot take files. Until one is signed, keep trade secrets out of what you tell me." If a file arrives, do not open or forward it, and reply once that you cannot take files yet. If they send their own NDA, do not sign it unread. [LAWYER]

**Once it exists.** Offer it after the call when files will help. No file moves before both have signed. First give your written answer on which services touch their files (C-021).

If a sender asks in an NDA or on a call about AI use or tools, answer honestly and in full, and never put a customer file into a free or personal AI account.

## 5. Scoping and quoting

Send within [2 working days, TO CONFIRM] of the call, one page:

> **Scope: [company], [stage].**
> - **Deliverables:** with formats ([CAD format, TO CONFIRM], PDF, STEP).
> - **Not included:** the stage's exclusions and anything dropped.
> - **You provide:** the stage's inputs by date, files only after the NDA. The clock starts when they are complete. Reviewer reply time [2 working days, TO CONFIRM].
> - **Review gates:** what you approve before I go on.
> - **Revisions:** [N] included, more quoted.
> - **Duration:** [YOUR HOURS] of work, [N] weeks from complete inputs.
> - **Price:** [YOUR PRICE], fixed or capped, excluding VAT.
> - **Payment:** [YOUR %] on acceptance, the rest on delivery. [LAWYER]
> - **Prototype parts and shipping:** you pay, at cost. [TO CONFIRM, LAWYER]
> - **Tooling and production parts:** manufacturers invoice you and you own the tooling. [TO CONFIRM, LAWYER]
> - **Manufacturers:** you approve the list before I send any file. [TO CONFIRM]
> - **Terms:** ownership on payment, changes, acceptance, case-study permission, liability. [LAWYER]

**Recommended for one engineer, with risk.**
- **Stage 1: fixed price.** Risk: hours are unlogged, so an overrun is yours.
- **Stage 2: hybrid.** Fixed for the listed deliverables, prototype parts at cost on top, extra rounds quoted. Risk: a board that is not really frozen, so re-quote if it moves.
- **Stage 3: hybrid.** Fixed for drawings and the request for quote pack, capped hours for design for manufacturing revisions, sample review and first batch support [limit TO CONFIRM]. Risk: they read the cap as the price, so warn before you reach it.

Time and materials with a cap alone carries that risk, and uncapped time and materials breaks the site's promise (C-009). Record every hour on the first two jobs. No parts resale or handling margin before insurance exists.

## 6. Follow up and capacity

**After the call.** Same day: send a recap and a dated next step. By [2 working days, TO CONFIRM]: send the scope. Two days after it, if silent, ask once whether it arrived and offer a short call. Day 7 after the call: one last note, offering a start slot only if you can hold it. Then mark it Parked and stop.

**Capacity.** The site says you take at most [N] projects at a time and say so early. Count accepted, undelivered projects before every reply. If full, say so in the first reply:

> Thank you for writing. I am at my maximum number of projects until about [date, TO CONFIRM], and I would rather say so now than let you wait. I can start on [date], or talk now so the scope is ready when I have room.

## 8. What to record from each inquiry

Add three columns: what brought them (the headline or section they mention, since the form has no source field), what the page did not answer (price, ownership, your location), and why it stopped, if it did. Fix the page when the same gap appears twice.

Keep personal data minimal: the company, not the person or address (the mailbox holds the thread), no product secrets, paraphrased notes. Blank the company 12 months after the last message (open question 27, [TO CONFIRM]) and keep counts.
