### Problem (#problem)
**Three moments a standard box stops fitting**

1. **The board is done and there is no box.** You have a working board and a ship date. The box has to match its connectors, antenna and mounting.
2. **The first box failed or costs too much.** A sealed box breathes. As it cools, the air inside contracts and draws humid air in through the weakest seal. A membrane vent equalizes pressure and blocks liquid water and dust, but passes water vapor, so condensation can still form. I start with the cheapest fix that works.
3. **From a few prototypes to hundreds of units.** Printing suits prototypes and small batches. In the hundreds and beyond, casting or molding can cost less per part. Tooling only pays off once volume covers it, and where that point falls depends on the part, so I ask for quotes.

### What I take on, and what I do not (#scope, added)
**I take on** housings for sensors, gateways, controllers, handheld and rail-mounted units, in sealed, outdoor and dusty conditions. I design to an ingress protection (IP) rating as defined in International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) standard 60529. The rating applies to the finished assembly with the glands, vent and cable I specify. The weakest opening sets the rating.

**Not for now:**
- Medical, automotive and defense products, and anything with a military use.
- Explosive atmospheres, including combustible dust.
- Products with 50 V AC or 75 V DC or more [TO CONFIRM].
- Simulation studies such as finite element analysis, airflow or mold filling. I build prototypes and measure them.
- Certification, marking and tests: electromagnetic compatibility (EMC), radio, flammability, ultraviolet (UV) exposure, thermal and ingress protection tests, CE marking and UL listing. I design for the rating you need. You or a test lab confirm it.
- Making production parts. I make printed prototypes only.
- Glands, vents and seals: I specify them, you buy them [TO CONFIRM].
